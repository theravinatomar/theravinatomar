Cross-Regional Recruitment Dashboard

Multiple duty stations across the South-East Asia region

Organization
World Health Organization, South-East Asia Regional Office (SEARO)
Scope
Multiple duty stations across the South-East Asia region
Role
Dashboard Designer & Recruitment Data Owner

Background & Context

Prior to this project, recruitment tracking across WHO SEARO's various duty stations was fragmented — each location maintained its own spreadsheets, timelines, and panel records with little standardization. This made it difficult for regional leadership to get an accurate, real-time view of hiring pipeline health, panel workload, budget exposure from open positions, or where delays were occurring. Reconciliation between duty stations was manual, slow, and error-prone, and budget planning around recruitment was hampered by inconsistent or outdated data. Leadership needed a single, reliable, centralized view of recruitment activity across the region to support better decision-making.

Project Aim

To design and deploy a centralized dashboard consolidating hiring timelines, interview panel activity, and recruitment outcomes across multiple duty stations — improving reconciliation accuracy, supporting budget planning, and materially increasing overall data accuracy.

My Involvement

I owned this project end-to-end: from initial requirements gathering through to design, build, deployment, and stakeholder adoption. This was largely an individually-driven initiative, requiring me to combine HR domain knowledge (understanding recruitment workflows and pain points) with data structuring and dashboard-building skills.

Key Tasks & Responsibilities

  • Requirements gatheringConsulted with hiring managers, recruitment panel coordinators, and HR colleagues across multiple duty stations to understand existing pain points, reporting needs, and the specific data points required for effective oversight.
  • Data audit and standardizationReviewed the inconsistent recruitment data formats used across duty stations and designed a standardized data schema that could accommodate every duty station's data while enabling direct comparison and aggregation.
  • Dashboard design and buildDesigned and built a centralized dashboard consolidating hiring pipeline stages and timelines per requisition, interview panel activity and composition, recruitment outcomes (offers extended, acceptances, declines, time-to-fill), and budget implications of open and in-progress recruitment actions.
  • Reconciliation process designEstablished a systematic reconciliation process to validate dashboard data against source recruitment records at each duty station, catching discrepancies before they propagated into regional reporting.
  • Data quality assuranceImplemented checks and validation routines to catch and correct inconsistent or incomplete data entries at the point of intake, rather than after the fact.
  • Stakeholder training and rolloutTrained HR colleagues and hiring managers across duty stations on how to use the dashboard, ensuring consistent data entry practices and driving adoption of the new centralized system.
  • Ongoing maintenance and iterationContinued to refine the dashboard based on user feedback, adding new views and metrics as leadership's reporting needs evolved.

Challenges Navigated

  • Resistance to standardization: some duty stations were accustomed to their own tracking methods and needed convincing that a centralized system would reduce, not add to, their workload.
  • Data quality at the source: many discrepancies uncovered during reconciliation stemmed from inconsistent data entry practices at the duty-station level, requiring both a technical fix (better validation) and a behavioral one (training and communication).
  • Balancing granularity with usability: the dashboard needed to be detailed enough for operational use while remaining simple enough for leadership to scan at a glance, requiring multiple rounds of stakeholder feedback.

Results & Outcomes

  • Improved reconciliation of recruitment data across duty stations, replacing manual, error-prone comparisons with a systematic, centralized process.
  • Enhanced budget planning capability, as leadership could now see consolidated hiring costs, timelines, and pipeline status in one place.
  • Improved overall data accuracy by 25%, a direct, measurable improvement in the reliability of recruitment reporting.
  • Gave regional leadership a single source of truth for recruitment performance, enabling faster, better-informed hiring decisions.
  • Established a replicable data standardization framework that could be extended to other HR data domains beyond recruitment.
