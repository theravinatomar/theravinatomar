Programme Financial & Timeline Dashboard

Workstream-level programme teams operating across borders

Organization
World Health Organization, South-East Asia Regional Office (SEARO)
Scope
Workstream-level programme teams operating across borders
Role
Dashboard Designer & Programme Tracking Lead

Background & Context

Cross-border programme teams at WHO SEARO were managing multiple workstreams simultaneously, each with its own timelines, funding allocations, and expenditure patterns. Without a consolidated tracking mechanism, programme leadership lacked real-time visibility into milestone progress, budget utilization, and where turnaround delays were occurring. Financial and timeline data was scattered across separate tools and spreadsheets maintained by different teams, making it difficult to spot risks early or reallocate resources proactively. This project was undertaken to build a single, workstream-level tracking tool that would give leadership and cross-border teams a unified, real-time view of programme health.

Project Aim

To build a workstream-level tracker that monitored timelines, funding allocation, and expenditure across programme workstreams — improving milestone visibility and reducing process turnaround time across cross-border teams.

My Involvement

I designed and built this tracker, working closely with programme leads and finance stakeholders to ensure it captured the right data points and served the practical reporting needs of teams operating across different countries and time zones.

Key Tasks & Responsibilities

  • Requirements definitionConsulted with programme leads and finance stakeholders to identify the specific timeline milestones, funding categories, and expenditure data points that needed to be tracked consistently across all workstreams.
  • Tracker architecture designDesigned the structure of the tracker to consolidate, at the workstream level: planned vs. actual milestone timelines, funding allocations by category and source, and actual expenditure against allocated budget — enabling variance analysis at a glance.
  • Build and deploymentBuilt the workstream-level tracker and deployed it for use across cross-border programme teams, ensuring it could be accessed and updated by team members in different locations.
  • Data update cadence and governanceEstablished regular data-update cycles and clear ownership for who was responsible for keeping timeline, funding, and expenditure data current within the tracker.
  • Milestone monitoring and escalationActively monitored milestone progress through the tracker, proactively flagging delays or budget variances to relevant programme leadership and stakeholders before they became critical issues.
  • Cross-team coordinationWorked with programme teams across different countries to ensure consistent understanding of tracker inputs and outputs, resolving discrepancies in how teams interpreted milestone or expenditure categories.
  • Iterative refinementCollected ongoing feedback from programme leads and cross-border teams, iterating on the tracker's design, layout, and metrics to improve usability and clarity of reporting over multiple revision cycles.
  • Reporting supportUsed the tracker's consolidated data to support leadership reporting on programme financial health and timeline adherence, reducing the manual effort previously required.

Challenges Navigated

  • Coordinating across time zones and teams: maintaining data currency and consistent update practices required clear governance and periodic follow-up to avoid stale or incomplete data.
  • Reconciling different budget and timeline conventions: different workstreams used different conventions for categorizing funding or defining milestones, requiring effort to establish a common framework.
  • Balancing detail with usability: the tracker needed enough granularity to be operationally useful while remaining simple enough for quick leadership review, requiring several rounds of design iteration.

Results & Outcomes

  • Improved milestone visibility for programme leadership and cross-border teams, replacing fragmented, team-level tracking with a single consolidated view.
  • Reduced process turnaround time by 15–20%, a direct and measurable efficiency gain from faster identification and resolution of timeline or funding issues.
  • Strengthened financial oversight and expenditure tracking across distributed workstreams, giving leadership better tools to manage budget risk proactively.
  • Enabled more consistent, data-driven communication between programme teams and leadership regarding programme health and resource needs.
